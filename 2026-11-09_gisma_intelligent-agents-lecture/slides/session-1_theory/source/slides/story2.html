What Happened in 1983

[NPR, 18 Sep 2017 · Smithsonian, 2017]

A Soviet satellite early-warning system reports five US missiles launched

Protocol: pass the warning up the chain of command

Stanislav Petrov doubts it: five missiles doesn’t look like a real first strike

He reports a malfunction. No missiles ever arrive

The “missiles” were sunlight reflecting off clouds.

The machine was confident. A human checked.

Not AI as we know it today: an automated detection system. The lesson is the same.

On 26 September 1983 the Soviet Oko early-warning system signalled five incoming US missiles. Petrov judged it a false alarm and did not pass it up the chain; about 23 minutes later no strike had arrived. The cause was sunlight reflecting off high clouds. Honest framing: this was an automated system, not modern AI, but it is the same question: who checks the machine?
