The ε-Greedy Strategy

[Murphy 2023, §35.1.5.1,
pp. 1136–1137]

29

Mostly exploit

Most rounds: play the option with the best record so far

Sometimes explore

Each round, with a small chance ε (say 10%): pick any option at random

ε is the dial

ε = the share of random rounds. Small: trust what you know. Large: keep experimenting.

Ten nights out, on average

Your favourite so far: exploit

A random pick from all the places you know: explore

In a research agent

Use the search source with the best record. Now and then, try another at random, in case it is better than its record suggests.

The catch

Explores blindly: a known-bad option gets the same random chance as a promising one.

Never stops: unless ε shrinks over time, about 1 round in 10 stays random.

Simple, and a solid baseline. Smarter: explore where you are unsure, with Thompson sampling.

The simplest answer to explore versus exploit. Each round, with a small probability epsilon, say 10%, the agent picks an option at random; otherwise it plays the option with the best record so far. The random pick is among the options it already has, and it can even land on the favourite. Over ten nights you expect about one random night; some runs of ten have none, some have two. Epsilon is the dial between trusting what you know and experimenting. Two weaknesses, both in Murphy’s book (§35.1.5.1): exploration is uniform, so a known-bad option gets the same random chance as a promising one; and it keeps exploring at the same rate unless epsilon is lowered over time. Still a solid baseline. Next: Thompson sampling, which explores where the agent is actually unsure.
