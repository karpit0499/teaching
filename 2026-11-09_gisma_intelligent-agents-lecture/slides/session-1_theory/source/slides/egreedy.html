The ε-Greedy Strategy

[Part 3 · Exploration strategies]

Mostly exploit

Most rounds: pick the option with the best record so far

Sometimes explore

Every so often, say 1 round in 10: pick any option at random

ε is the dial

Small: trust what you know. Large: keep experimenting.

In a research agent

Use the search source with the best track record. Now and then, try another one at random to check it hasn’t got better.

Ten nights out

Your favourite restaurant: exploit

A random new place: explore

The catch

Explores blindly: a place you know is bad gets tried as often as a promising one.

Never stops: unless ε shrinks over time, 1 night in 10 is always a gamble.

Simple, and a solid baseline. Smarter: explore where you are unsure → Thompson sampling.

The simplest fix for explore vs exploit. Most of the time do what has worked best so far; a small fraction of the time, epsilon, pick at random. Restaurant version: nine nights out of ten your favourite, one night a random new place. Epsilon is the dial between trusting what you know and experimenting. Two weaknesses: it explores blindly, giving a known-bad option the same random chance as a promising one, and it never stops exploring unless epsilon is lowered over time. Still a strong baseline, and a fair first answer in an exam. Next: Thompson sampling, which explores where the agent is actually unsure.
