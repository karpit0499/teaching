Beliefs With Confidence

[Murphy 2023, §3.2, pp. 65–72]

18

Good agents hold beliefs, not facts

Every estimate carries a degree of confidence

Stating a guess as a fact is a classic agent failure

Bayes: update beliefs with evidence

Prior: what you believed before

Evidence: what you just observed

Posterior: the updated belief

Uncertainty tells the agent what to do

A wide range says: check the live price first

It also warns the user the figure is an estimate

Sounds certain

“The hotel costs 90 € a night.”

Honest belief

“Probably about 90 €, from last year’s listing. Could be 80–120 €.”

An agent should hold beliefs with degrees of confidence. The second statement leads to better decisions: it tells the agent that checking the current price may be worthwhile and warns the user. Bayesian statistics, PML2 §3.2, describes how a prior is combined with data into a posterior.
